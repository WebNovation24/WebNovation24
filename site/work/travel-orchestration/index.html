Case study · Travel orchestration SaaS

Multi-tenant authority and critical workflow state

A modular multi-tenant travel operations system that separates authentication, business authorization, published traveler-facing state and critical workflow outcomes.

Central challenge

How do you preserve authority when a multi-tenant workflow crosses authentication, permissions, versioned business state, external operations and the browser?

Constraints

Multi-tenancy · Authentication ≠ authorization · Defense in depth · Unknown outcomes · Release discipline · Private source repository.

Context

Travel operations combine commercial state, itinerary work, publication, traveler access and later changes. The interface may know an action was initiated while the authoritative business result still belongs to the server, database or external provider.

Public-safe authority model
Authentication — establish session identity
↓ does not grant business capability by itself
Business authorization — organization scope, memberships, capabilities
↓ reinforced at the data layer
PostgreSQL / Supabase RLS — defense in depth
↓ critical operation may remain uncertain
Reconcile authoritative outcome — UNKNOWN stays distinct from success

Technical decisions

Three decisions show how identity, state authority and release verification stay explicit across the system.

01

Authentication is not business authorization

Problem
An authenticated session proves identity, but does not by itself define organization scope or business capability.
Constraint
Multi-tenant data and actions must stay scoped to explicit business authority, and UI visibility cannot serve as access control.
Decision
Separate authentication/session ownership from business identity and authorization, with database RLS as defense in depth.
Implementation
Better Auth owns session concerns while the product owns organizations, memberships, roles/capabilities and server-authoritative access decisions.
Verification
Repository documentation covers integration testing and pgTAP/RLS validation within the local verification gate.
02

Unknown is a first-class outcome

Problem
External operations can leave the client uncertain after a timeout, interrupted navigation or provider return.
Constraint
Treating uncertainty as success can expose incorrect business state; blind retry can duplicate operations.
Decision
Represent uncertain outcomes explicitly and reconcile against authoritative state before declaring success.
Implementation
The product contract distinguishes operation state, authoritative result and unknown/conflict outcomes.
Verification
The invariant is explicit in the repository’s current product authority.
03

Make release evidence reproducible

Problem
A multi-package SaaS can pass isolated checks while failing at data, build or browser boundaries.
Constraint
Release evidence needs to cross several layers and stay tied to a reproducible candidate.
Decision
Use a local verification gate spanning static checks, database/RLS validation, build and browser E2E.
Implementation
The repository exposes a canonical local verification workflow and explicit environment boundaries.
Verification
README/scripts document the gate scope and staging separation without claiming universal environmental proof.
Evidence / verification
Public-safe evidence supports modular architecture boundaries, authentication separated from business authorization, PostgreSQL/Supabase RLS as defense in depth, a reproducible multi-layer local gate, logical staging separation and unknown outcomes represented separately from success. It does not support real-traveler adoption or business-impact claims.
Observable outcome

Authority has an explicit place at every layer.

The observable technical result is a multi-tenant system whose architecture gives explicit places to authentication, business authorization, database-level access controls, uncertain workflow outcomes and release verification.
